The one idea this whole site rests on

A benchmark score is a measurement, not a promise: one test, one group of people, one moment in time.

That doesn't make it useless — a flashlight doesn't stop being useful just because it isn't flood lighting. It means you treat every banner number as the start of a question, not the end of one.

Five questions to ask any big score

  1. What exactly was measured?
  2. Was the test fresh or famous? (Old, public benchmarks can be memorized — contamination.)
  3. Is everyone clustered at 90%+? Then the test has stopped separating good from very good.
  4. What's in each slice? Per-category scores hide behind the average.
  5. Who ran it, how? Prompt phrasing changes scores; scores are estimates, not laws of nature.

What this is not

This is education, not endorsement — of any model, vendor, or hype cycle. Nothing here tells you which model to buy; it teaches you how to ask better questions and run your own quick checks. And it is never medical, legal, or financial advice: treat anything an AI tells you like a draft from a new intern — check the details with sources you trust, especially where health, money, or law are involved.
