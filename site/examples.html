2. Decode a name you've never seen

Benchmark names are proud, slightly silly acronyms. The three-move decoder:

  1. Pick the domain (math, questions, common sense).
  2. Add scope words — Massive, Multi-, Grade-School, Graduate-Level.
  3. Compress into something pronounceable, ideally cute.

MMLU = Massive Multitask Language Understanding. Massive (thousands of items), Multitask (many subjects mixed), Language Understanding (the aspiration, not the proof).

Decode the suffixes — they carry half the meaning.
You seeUsual meaning
-Pro, -Hard, -XXL, -8KHarder or bigger edition
-VerifiedHumans filtered item quality
Multi-, M(M)Many subjects / includes images
Live, Fresh, Held-outCreated after training — anti-memorization
Arena, EloHuman votes, not percent correct

Practice with a fictional one: NurseQA-Hard-Multi: 90%. You already know the shape: question-answer items, harder edition, several topics mixed — and per the ceiling rule, "everyone likely passed." Full decoder in docs/03-benchmark-names-decoded.md.

The banner says Storyteller wins. The slices say: depends what you need. Three standing rules:

  • A truthful score is not a rate of lies per sentence — read what unit the percent is of.
  • High truthfulness on known folklore transfers weakly to new topics.
  • Lower hallucination never means zero. Verify what matters.

5. Cloud vs. local, decided honestly

The deciding questions, in order — most people answer four, and the first one settles it:

  1. Privacy? If you'd blush seeing the prompt in a neighbor's newsletter, keep it local.
  2. Hardware? 8 GB → tiny models; 16 GB → mid-small (7–8B, the sweet spot); 32 GB+ → lower-mid (13–14B). M-series Macs punch above their RAM line. Rules of thumb, not laws.
  3. Tasks? Everyday drafting, rewriting, summarizing, brainstorming → small local models usually suffice. Hard multi-step reasoning over big documents → cloud (or a hybrid).
  4. Always-available? Local needs no network, subscription, or rate limit — but model quality is capped by your machine.

"Ollama-friendly" means: has a GGUF build, fits your RAM, is a chat/instruct model, and has a license you can live with.
