Arena rating (Chatbot Arena, Elo-style)
A leaderboard built from real people voting between two anonymous answers. Measures preference, not correctness — polished, confident answers tend to win votes. Meaningful only within the same pool of rated models.
Benchmark
A fixed set of tasks with known answers (or a judging method), used to score a model. A measurement, not a promise: one test, one moment, one set of assumptions.
Benchmark saturation
When top models cluster near the top score and small differences (91.2 vs. 91.8) are mostly noise. The polite reading: "everyone passed."
Chat / instruct model
A model tuned to converse and follow instructions — what you want for everyday use. A "base" model, by contrast, just continues text and will happily babble.
Contamination
Benchmark questions leaking into training data, so a model can score from memory rather than skill. Like grading a test someone already saw in study hall. Look for "held-out" or "fresh" items.
Cloud model
A model running on a provider's servers; you send prompts over the network and pay a fee. Convenient and capable; data goes somewhere else's computers.
Context window
How much text a model can consider at once. Exceed it and the model genuinely cannot "see" the extra material — no amount of talent fixes that.
Elo rating
A relative score from pairwise comparisons, borrowed from chess. Tells you who beat whom within one rated pool — not an absolute measure of goodness.
GGUF
The common file format for compressed local model files; standard for Ollama and friends. Its size on disk is a good preview of what your computer must carry.
Hallucination
A confident, fluent answer that is wrong — invented citations, wrong dates, fake specifics. A natural side effect of generating plausible text, not a random glitch. Plausible is not the same as true.
Held-out / fresh items
Benchmark questions kept out of training (or created after training ended) to resist memorization. A good sign whenever a leaderboard mentions them.
Inference
Running a trained model to get answers — what happens every time you press send, as opposed to training, which happens on the builder's side.
Jailbreak
A prompt trick attempting to bypass a model's safety behavior. Safety scores only cover the tricks known when the test was built; new ones appear regularly.
LLM (large language model)
The model type behind chat assistants: trained on enormous amounts of text to predict plausible continuations. Clever, and sometimes confidently wrong in exactly the way that implies.
Ollama
A popular free tool for downloading and running local models on your own computer with simple commands. "Local-first" for people who like clean tools.
Local model
A model running on your own hardware. Private by default; limited by your machine's RAM and speed — and, honestly, none of your data ever leaves your desk.
MMLU (Massive Multitask Language Understanding)
Thousands of multiple-choice questions across school subjects; the classic "general-knowledge survey" benchmark. Famous, widely used, and old enough to stare at very high scores suspiciously.
Multimodal
Handles more than one input type — text plus images, sometimes audio. Acronyms with M(M) in the middle are usually the image cousins of text tests.
Over-refusal
Declining harmless requests out of caution. High "refusal rate" is not automatically a virtue: a model that refuses everything is safe and useless at the same time.
Parameters (B = billion)
Roughly, the model's size. More parameters means more capability headroom and more RAM required; smaller isn't worse so much as different.
Per-category (per-slice) scores
The sub-scores behind an overall average. An overall 78% can hide a 95% and a 41% — read the slices whenever they're published.
Prompt
The text you send the model. Its phrasing measurably changes scores, which is why identical benchmarks run by different teams produce different numbers.
Quantization
Compressing a model's internal numbers so it fits consumer hardware, trading a little quality for a lot of feasibility. The reason a 13B-class model can live on a 16 GB laptop.
RAM
Your computer's working memory — the main gatekeeper for local models. Rough ladder: 8 GB → tiny models; 16 GB → mid-small (7–8B); 32 GB+ → lower-mid (13–14B). Rules of thumb, not laws.
Refusal rate
How often a model declines requests. Always read it per-category: declining harmful prompts is good; declining harmless ones is a bug.
Safety benchmark
A test of how a model responds to adversarial prompts (bias probes, toxicity, harmful requests). A snapshot against known attacks — not a seal of approval.
SOTA (state of the art)
"Best publicly known score right now" — a title that quietly expires every few weeks. Great for headlines, useless as a label on a printed page.
TruthfulQA-style benchmark
Tests whether a model resists common misconceptions ("we use 10% of our brains…") instead of echoing them. Measures resistance to folklore, not total factual accuracy.
